    Floors, roofs, stairs and components

    Floors, roofs, stairs and railings are drawn in sketch mode: their lines are sketched first, and the element is made only when the sketch is finished. Components (furniture and plumbing fixtures) are placed with a click. Curtain walls are described in Walls, doors and windows.

    Sketch mode

    Home ▸ Build ▸ Flooring, Roof, Stairs and Railing open sketch mode. The contextual tab is named after what is sketched (Edit | Create Floor Boundary, Create Roof Footprint, Create Stairs Sketch, Create Railing Path) and has:

    • the Mode panel: Finish Edit Mode makes the element; Cancel Edit Mode drops the sketch and leaves the model as it was;
    • the Draw panel, with the drawing tools of the element;
    • for stairs and railings, the Tools panel.

    Nothing is made while sketching: the lines are magenta, and the model changes only with Finish Edit Mode. The whole element is then one step of the undo history: one Ctrl+Z takes it away.

    Drawing lines

    Tool How it draws
    Line Click point after point: each click ends a line and starts the next. The length and angle of the line under the cursor are shown beside it; Shift keeps it horizontal or vertical. With Continue off on the options bar, each line is drawn on its own. Clicking the start of the chain again closes the loop Type a length and press Enter: the line ends that far along the cursor's direction (a stair's run too).
    Rectangle Two opposite corners: four lines
    Pick Walls Click a wall: its line is added to the sketch (see below). Press Tab over a wall, then click, to pick the whole chain of walls joined end to end
    Pick Lines Move over a wall, a grid, a line or the edge of a floor or roof: the line under the cursor is highlighted and named (a wall's faces, each layer boundary, its two Core Boundary lines and its axis); Tab goes to the next one there. Click to add a sketch line on it (set off by Offset towards the cursor)
    Slope Arrow Click the tail, then the head of the arrow (floors and roofs)

    Offset on the options bar moves the drawn lines (and the lines of picked walls) by that distance.

    The arcs of the Draw panel (and a circle, as two halves) are one sketch line each: picked, measured and dragged as arcs, kept by the floor, roof, stair, wall boundary or filled region, and given back as arcs by Edit Boundary or Edit Sketch. In every view a sketch point snaps to the ends and middles of the sketch's lines and arcs, to arcs' centres, where lines cross and onto a line or an arc; from the point being drawn from, to the foot of a perpendicular and to the point where a line touches an arc. The marker and its name show where the point snaps; S C and S P ask for the centre or the perpendicular for the next point.

    Floors and roofs need closed loops. A loop drawn inside the outer one is an opening: the floor or the roof has a hole there. When Finish Edit Mode finds lines that do not close or that cross, a message says so and the sketch stays open to be fixed (Continue).

    Picking and changing sketch lines

    Esc ends the chain being drawn; a second Esc puts the drawing tool down (so does Modify). While the sketch is open its lines are elements of the view, and Modify works on them as on other lines; nothing else can be picked until the sketch is finished:

    • the line under the cursor is highlighted; a click selects it, Ctrl adds lines, Shift takes them away, a window selects several, Tab the chain of joined lines;
    • the end of a line selected on its own drags alone: the line next to it stays where it is and the join is broken; with a chain selected (Tab), the ends where its lines meet drag together; a dragged line takes the lines joined to it along;
    • Move, Copy, Rotate, Mirror on Line, Repeat in Series, Align, Offset, Shorten/Lengthen and Split Element work on them, and the sketch stays open;
    • one selected line shows temporary dimensions to the nearest parallel line on each side (another sketch line, the wall's original outline, a wall, a grid, a level): click the value and type a distance to move the line; the symbol beside it makes a permanent dimension, kept while the sketch is open;
    • its properties are in the Properties palette: Defines Slope and Slope, for a roof line picked from a wall its Overhang, and its Length.

    Align takes as its reference the line under the cursor, and Tab goes to the next one there, so a sketch line can be aligned to a wall's layer or core boundary. While drawing or dragging an end, the points snap to the corners of the walls' faces, layers and core boundaries, then onto those lines. Del deletes the selected lines. Ctrl+Z while sketching takes back the last change to the sketch, not to the model. All this works in plans, in sections and elevations (a wall's boundary) and, for selecting, dragging ends and deleting, in the 3D view.

    Editing the sketch of an element

    Select a floor, a roof, a stair or a railing: the Mode panel of its contextual tab has Edit Boundary (floors), Edit Footprint (roofs), Edit Sketch (stairs) or Edit Path (railings). Sketch mode opens with the element's lines, openings, slopes and arrow; Finish Edit Mode changes the same element, Cancel Edit Mode leaves it unchanged.

    The sketch opens with Modify active: its lines can be selected and changed at once.

    In a plan the sketch is drawn on the level; in the 3D view it lies on the element's own plane (the floor's top, the roof's base) and the 3D view stays open.

    A new floor can be sketched in the 3D view too: Flooring started there puts the sketch on the plane of the level, the line being drawn follows the cursor with its length, and Finish Edit Mode makes the floor without leaving the 3D view. In the 3D view the options bar's Level chooses the work plane: the level whose plane the sketch lies on and the floor goes on. Pick Walls takes the wall whose face is under the cursor. Roofs by footprint, stairs and railings are sketched in a plan.

    Floors

    1. Click Home ▸ Build ▸ Flooring. Sketch mode starts with Pick Walls.
    2. Click the walls around the floor, or press Tab over one of them and click to pick them all. Each line goes on the interior face of its wall; the lines of walls that meet are trimmed or extended to meet at the corner.
    3. Or draw the boundary with Line or Rectangle; draw a loop inside it for an opening.
    4. Click Finish Edit Mode.

    On the options bar, Extend into wall (to core) puts picked lines into the wall up to the face of its core, and Offset moves them (a negative offset moves them away from the walls). The type of the new floor is the one in the type selector at the top of Properties.

    Sloped floors

    A floor slopes in one direction, set in one of two ways while sketching:

    • a Slope Arrow: its properties are Specify (Height at Tail, from the heights of its two ends, or Slope), Height Offset at Tail, Height Offset at Head and Slope. The heights are measured from the top of the floor;
    • one boundary line with Defines Slope ticked and its Slope in degrees: the floor rises from that line, inwards.

    The floor is sloped in 3D and in sections. Its Slope Angle in Properties changes the slope afterwards.

    Floors over walls

    When a floor crosses walls (an upper floor resting on the walls below, for instance), its slab is cut out of them: in 3D and in sections the wall stops under the floor and goes on above it, and walls go on through the floor's openings.

    Floor properties

    Property What it is
    Level, Height Offset From Level The level and the height of the top face from it
    Slope Angle The slope of a sloped floor, in degrees
    Thickness From the type's layers (read-only)
    Perimeter, Area, Volume Without the openings (read-only)
    Comments, Mark Free text

    Type Parameters has the floor's Structure (its layers) and its default thickness.

    Roofs

    1. Click Home ▸ Build ▸ Roof. Sketch mode starts with Pick Walls; the options bar has Defines Slope, Overhang and Extend into wall (to core).
    2. Pick the walls (Tab for the whole chain). Each line goes on the exterior face of its wall, moved out by the overhang (from the face of the core with Extend into wall (to core)).
    3. Or draw the footprint with Line or Rectangle; a loop inside is an opening.
    4. Click Finish Edit Mode.

    Every line with Defines Slope rises inwards at its Slope (30° by default); a small triangle with the slope is drawn beside it. The shape comes from those lines:

    • all the lines define the slope: a hip roof;
    • two opposite lines do not: a gable roof, the walls under the gables rise to the ridge when attached;
    • no line defines the slope: a flat roof, or a roof sloping along a Slope Arrow (then no line may define a slope).

    To change one line, press Esc twice, click the line and change Defines Roof Slope, Slope or (lines picked from walls) Overhang in Properties.

    Select walls, click Modify Wall ▸ Link Top/Base, then the roof: they rise to its underside, gables included (Walls, doors and windows tells more).

    Roof properties

    Property What it is
    Base Level, Base Offset From Level Where the eaves are: by default on top of the walls of the level
    Slope The slope of every line that defines one (or of the arrow)
    Thickness, Maximum Ridge Height, Area, Volume Read-only
    Comments, Mark Free text

    In plan a roof shows its outline, ridges and hips; in 3D its slopes, overhangs and layers.

    Stairs

    1. Click Home ▸ Vertical Links ▸ Stairs. Sketch mode starts with Run.
    2. Click where the first riser is, then where the run ends: the run snaps to whole treads, and the status bar counts the risers made and left (for example 9 risers created, 8 remaining).
    3. To turn, click again away from the end of the first run and draw the next one: a landing joins them. At any angle the landing is bounded by the two runs' risers, its outer corner where their outer sides meet.
    4. When every riser is placed, click Finish Edit Mode.

    Dashed guides show where the next run can start: the last run's extension beyond its end and the lines of its first and last risers. A click near them snaps onto them. In a 3D view the stair is drawn the same way, on the plane of the active level.

    A curved run (a spiral stair): with Run active, click Center-ends arc, then the centre, the start of the run line (its distance from the centre is the radius of the walking line) and the end, going round past half a turn if needed, but under one turn (further, the run stops short and the status bar warns that the runs would overlap). The risers radiate from the centre a tread apart on the run line; riser lines drawn radiating from a centre make a curved run too. A second curved run round the same centre and on the same run line (a click near them takes them) is joined to the first by a landing along the arc.

    A stair can also be drawn by its lines. In its sketch, click Draw ▸ Boundary and draw the left and right edges (green), then Riser and draw the riser lines (black) between them, with Line or Pick Lines. Parallel, evenly spaced risers make a run, as wide as the risers; a landing joins two runs out to the boundary lines. Each riser is as high as the storey divided by the desired number of risers; with fewer riser lines the stair stops short of the top (Properties shows the actual number). Edit Sketch gives the same lines back. Each run drawn with Run is at once boundary and riser lines like these: they can be moved or deleted like the others. A landing follows its boundary lines: split its far edge with Split Element and drag a piece to notch it, or draw it wider than the runs.

    The Width on the options bar is that of the new stair (by default the type's). Tools ▸ Railing Type chooses the railings of the new stair: a railing type, put on both sides, or None.

    The number of risers comes from the height between Base Level and Top Level (with their offsets) and the type's Maximum Riser Height; changing a level or an offset in Properties changes the risers. Edit Sketch reopens its boundary and riser lines: change or delete them (click, Del) and draw runs again; the railings of the stair follow. Multistory Top Level repeats the stair, with its railings, storey by storey up to that level; a storey whose height is not the stair's gets no copy, and a warning names it. With the stair selected, the blue flip arrows beside its first riser make it go up from the other end; Up Label, Up Arrow, Down Label and Down Arrow in Properties show or hide the plan symbol's parts. The square grip under the UP text drags it elsewhere.

    In plan the stair shows its treads up to the cut line, the break line, the arrow and the UP/DN text; in 3D its treads and risers, or a monolithic slab, its landings and railings.

    How a stair is built

    Type Parameters of the stair decide its construction:

    • Monolithic Stairs ticked: each run is a concrete slab cut to the steps; its underside runs parallel to the slope, Structural Depth below the inner corners of the steps, and the landings are as thick: the sloping underside of a run meets the landing's underside along a line, without a step; each run ends with its last tread: the landing, flush with it, or the floor above makes the last riser, so steps and slab show as one solid with no line across the risers.
    • otherwise treads (Tread Thickness) with risers (Riser Type Straight, Riser Thickness) or open (None), on stringers: Right Stringer and Left Stringer Closed (a board the treads are housed in), Open (a board cut to the steps, the treads on top) or None, and Middle Stringers under the treads, with their Stringer Thickness and Stringer Height.
    • Begin with Riser and End with Riser cleared: the board of the stair's first or last riser is not built.
    • Riser Type Slanted leans each riser board, its top under the front of the tread above; Riser to Tread Connection runs each tread under the riser behind it (Extend Tread Under Riser) or the riser down behind the tread (Extend Riser Behind Tread).
    • Nosing Length sets how far a tread runs past its riser; Extend Below Base moves the foot of the stringers (negative: below the base level); Tread Material, Riser Material, Stringer Material and Monolithic Material colour the parts; Break Symbol in Plan and Text Size control the plan symbol.

    The landing stops behind the last riser of the run that reaches it, following its back face; in a monolithic stair it starts at the riser's face. In a monolithic stair whose top floor has its edge on the last riser, the run's underside goes on under that floor until it meets the floor's underside, as under a landing.

    A section across the stair shows it cut with its slab. Volume in Properties and in the schedule of stairs is the volume of its solids.

    The type's Text Font, Nosing Profile (Default or Rounded) and Apply Nosing Profile (front only, front and left, or front, left and right: past a side without a closed stringer), Open Stringer Offset, Trim Stringers at Top (Match Level stops the side stringers at the top level) and its identification (Code, Model, Manufacturer, Type Comments, URL, Description, Assembly Code) are in Type Parameters, as is a monolithic stair's Underside of Stringer (Smooth, a sloping slab, or Stepped: the slab follows the steps). Under Calculation Rules, Use Stair Calculator with a Target, Maximum and Minimum Result checks 2 risers + 1 tread: a stair outside the range is made, and a warning says so.

    Ramps

    1. Click Home ▸ Vertical Links ▸ Ramp. Sketch mode starts with Run.
    2. Click where a run starts, then where it ends: it slopes at most the type's Ramp Max Slope (1/x) and rises at most its Maximum Incline Length (while drawing, a longer run stops there); the length made and left show beside the cursor.
    3. Draw the next run away from the end of the first: a landing joins them. Boundary and Riser draw a ramp by its lines, with a riser line at the start and the end of each run.
    4. Click Finish Edit Mode.

    The type's Shape is Thick (its Thickness) or Solid (down to the base), and Ramp Material colours it. Its railings, Edit Sketch and Properties work as for stairs.

    With Arc from Center and End in the Draw panel, click the centre, the start of the run line and then its end round the centre: the run curves round it, two lines radiating from the centre at its ends and two boundary arcs at its sides. The type's Show Up Arrow in All Views draws the whole up arrow and UP in the plan of the top level too; its Text Font and identification are as for stairs.

    Railings

    1. Click Home ▸ Vertical Links ▸ Railing.
    2. Draw the path with Line: one chain of connected lines (a path in pieces, a closed path or one that goes back over itself is refused at Finish Edit Mode).
    3. To put the railing on a stair, click Tools ▸ Pick New Host and then the stair: the path drawn on it climbs with the stair.
    4. Click Finish Edit Mode.

    Edit Path reopens the path of a railing. The railing's Type Parameters has its Railing Height and Baluster Spacing: changing them changes every railing of that type.

    The railings of a stair run on over its landings as one rail, at their height above the line of the nosings and above the landings. Where a run leaves a landing one riser higher, the type's Tangent Joins (the rail goes straight on) and Angled Joins (it turns) decide the join: Extend Rails to Meet (tangent joins only) carries the sloping rail on down to the landing, Add Vertical/Horizontal Segments rises upright at the first riser, No Connector leaves a gap.

    The rails are mitred at every joint, where the path turns and where the slope changes: with Rail Connections Weld each rail is one piece, with Trim its pieces are cut on the joint's vertical plane. A post stands at the start and the end of each railing and at every change of direction in plan and wherever the slope changes, as where a run meets a landing. Use Landing Height Adjustment with Landing Height Adjustment raises (or lowers) the rail over the landings by that much.

    Components

    1. Click Home ▸ Build ▸ Component. The type selector lists the components loaded in the project; a preview follows the cursor, in plan and in the 3D view.
    2. Press Space to turn the preview by 90°.
    3. Click to place the component on the level of the plan.

    Mode ▸ Load Family opens the library, by folder (Furniture, Plumbing Fixtures): choose a family and click Open to add its types to the project.

    Select a component and click Host ▸ Pick New Host, then a floor: the component moves onto the floor and stays on its top (click an empty point to put it back on the level). Its properties are Level, Host, Offset from Level, Rotation, Mark and Comments; Type Parameters has its width, depth, height and material.

    Schedules and IFC

    Floors, roofs, stairs, railings and furniture have their schedules (Views ▸ Create ▸ Schedules ▸ Schedule/Quantities), one row per element. Export ▸ Export IFC4… writes floors as slabs (with their openings and slopes), roofs, stairs with their flights and landings, railings, furniture and plumbing fixtures. A multistory stair is written once in each storey it is repeated in, with its railings.

    Not yet in BIMopen
